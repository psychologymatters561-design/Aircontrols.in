What’s at stake

Nobody notices office AC until the 3 pm meeting moves floors.

An office AC estate fails in inconvenient instalments: the corner cabin that never cools, the meeting room that hits 30°C with eight people in it, the server closet that quietly climbs until hardware starts throttling. Each one lands on the admin desk as an urgent complaint — and in June, every contractor’s queue is already full of them.

There is a cost meter running even without complaints: a fouled coil makes a compressor draw 15–30% more power, and offices run tens of units for 8–10 hours a day. Deferred cleaning is a permanent surcharge on the electricity bill — the arithmetic is here.

How the contract runs in an office

Zoned scheduling
Cabins and meeting rooms serviced in rotation during the day; open-floor cassettes and ducted plant off-hours. No floor loses cooling all at once.
Server & UPS rooms
Round-the-clock units on quarterly or monthly checks, with condensate and redundancy verified — these are the units that cannot wait for the pre-summer visit.
Complaint-to-dispatch
Breakdowns carry the written 2-hour commercial response, so “the AC in 4B is dead” has an answer with a timestamp.
Reporting for management
Per-unit condition reports and a prioritised action list — the admin team’s evidence that the estate is managed, not merely attended.

FAQ

Specifics for this sector.

Can servicing happen outside office hours?

Yes, and for open floors it should. Cabin splits can be serviced zone by zone during the day; cassettes and ducted plant over open desks are done before 9 or after close. We work 7:00 AM to 10:00 PM, seven days a week, so the calendar fits around your business day.

We have five cabin splits and a server-room unit. Is that too small for an AMC?

No. Small offices are standard contract work — and the server-room unit alone usually justifies the contract, because it runs around the clock and fails without drama until the rack alarms go off. High-duty units go on a tighter visit schedule than the comfort units.

What does the admin team actually receive?

A visit calendar, a per-unit written condition report each cycle, and a prioritised action list with budget implications — the document you forward to management when they ask why the AC budget is what it is. Plus GST invoicing and vendor documentation for the procurement file.

Our lease says the landlord maintains the ducted plant. What about our own units?

Common situation. The contract covers whatever estate is yours — cabin and server splits, cassettes you installed — and the condition reports give you evidence if the landlord-maintained plant is what’s actually failing your floor.
