The multi-unit problem

Forty ACs is not forty small jobs. It’s one system.

Past a handful of units, ad-hoc servicing stops working: nobody knows which units were done, which are drifting, or which will fail first. The fix is not more phone calls — it is an inventory, a calendar and a paper trail.

  1. Inventory and tag

    Every unit gets an ID: location, type, tonnage, age, serial. A 40-unit building becomes 40 traceable histories.

  2. Baseline assessment

    Readings on every unit before the contract starts — so “improved” and “deteriorating” mean something later. This is the facility assessment.

  3. Zone scheduling

    Services run zone by zone, outside working hours where the floor demands it — server rooms and reception don’t go down together.

  4. Consolidated reporting

    One report per cycle for the whole estate: per-unit readings, condition ratings and a prioritised action list your management can budget from.

  5. Breakdown SLA

    2-hour commercial response in writing, a named contact and an escalation matrix above them.

Scope of every service

The 9-Point Protocol

The same nine measurements on a 6-unit clinic and a 200-unit campus. Scale changes the schedule, never the standard.

  • 01Evaporator coilCleaned; condition rated and recorded
  • 02Condenser coilCleaned, fins straightened; condition rated
  • 03Air filterServiced or replaced; airflow restored
  • 04Condensate drainFlushed and cleared; flow verified
  • 05Electrical connectionsChecked and tightened; terminals inspected
  • 06CapacitorMicrofarad value measured and recorded against rating
  • 07Refrigerant pressureMeasured against manufacturer specification, recorded
  • 08Fan motor & bladeWear, bearing noise and balance inspected
  • 09ThermostatCalibration tested against measured air temperature

FAQ

Commercial contracts — specifics.

How do you run an AMC across a large, multi-unit site?

It starts with an inventory: every unit tagged with an ID, location, type, tonnage and age. Service visits are scheduled zone by zone — outside your working hours where needed — and each unit’s readings go into its own history. Facility managers get one consolidated report per cycle, not forty loose job cards.

Can you work outside office hours?

Yes. Open-floor and customer-facing areas are typically serviced before opening or after close; we operate 7:00 AM to 10:00 PM, seven days a week, so scheduled work does not collide with your business day.

Who do we deal with during the contract?

A named point of contact for the site, with a written escalation path above them. Breakdown calls go to dispatch, not to a personal mobile that may or may not answer.

What does procurement get?

GST invoicing, a written scope-of-work, the service calendar, the SLA and escalation matrix, and per-cycle condition reports for your files. Vendor documentation is available from the contact page.

Our units are spread across several sites. One contract or several?

One contract can cover multiple sites with a per-site schedule and consolidated reporting — common for retail chains and offices with branch locations across Delhi NCR.

What is the corporate AC AMC price for an office in Delhi?

Quoted per unit per year after a site assessment — driven by unit type, tonnage, age, count and cover level. Multi-unit sites price better per unit than small ones. The pricing guide shows the factors with numbers.
