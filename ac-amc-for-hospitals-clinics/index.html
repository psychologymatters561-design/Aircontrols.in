What’s at stake

In healthcare, cooling is a clinical parameter.

Operating and procedure rooms, medicine storage, laboratory and imaging equipment — each holds a temperature band that is not negotiable in a Delhi June. An AC failure in a clinic is not discomfort; it is postponed procedures, stressed equipment and stock in the pharmacy fridge line at risk.

The second issue is the air itself. A neglected AC breathes through a wet, dirty coil and a stagnant drain pan — exactly the biology you do not want distributed into clinical space. Hygiene is why the coil grade and drain check are recorded per unit, per visit, not assumed.

FAQ

Specifics for this sector.

Can you service clinical areas without disrupting operations?

Yes — that constraint shapes the whole schedule. Patient-facing and procedure areas are serviced in agreed windows, coordinated with your facility team, with clean work practices and units returned to duty the same session. Waiting areas and admin zones rotate separately.

Why does coil cleaning matter more in a clinic than an office?

The evaporator coil and drain pan sit wet in the airstream all summer — left dirty, they harbour mould and bacteria that the unit then distributes into the room. In clinical spaces that is an air-quality issue, not just an efficiency issue. Coil condition is graded and recorded at every visit.

Pharmacies and diagnostic equipment need tight temperature bands. How is that handled?

Temperature-critical rooms are treated as high-duty zones: tighter visit schedules, drift checked against the band the room must hold, and priority dispatch if a unit fails. The condition report flags any unit trending toward the edge of its band before it crosses it.

Do you provide documentation for audits and inspections?

Yes. Every visit produces written per-unit records — what was serviced, what was measured, what was found — which slot directly into facility audit files. The report format is here.
