The territory

The Okhla Industrial Area — Phases I, II and III, with the NSIC complex and the Okhla Industrial Estate alongside — is South Delhi’s working district: printing and packaging houses, garment exporters, electronics and light engineering units, food processors, and increasingly the media offices, studios and design firms that have taken over renovated factory floors. Behind almost every gate is the same combination — a production or storage floor, an office block, and an AC estate that grew piecemeal as the business did.

Air Control’s base in East of Kailash is ten to fifteen minutes up the road, past Nehru Place. Okhla has been part of our working territory for decades, and it is some of the hardest duty air conditioning sees anywhere in Delhi.

What Okhla’s stock asks of AC maintenance

  • The harshest condenser environment in South Delhi. Industrial exhaust, road dust from the arterial crossings and packed rooftop plant mean condenser coils here foul at multiples of the residential-colony rate. Left alone, that is a 15–30% power surcharge per affected unit and early compressor death. Cleaning cycles in Okhla are set shorter, from measured coil grades — not from a citywide average.
  • Process spaces with real stakes. Print floors that must hold paper in register, electronics assembly, sample rooms holding fabric and colour standards, and server rooms running production software — spaces where temperature drift costs output, not comfort. These units go on high-duty schedules with drift tracked visit to visit; genuinely temperature-critical storage belongs with cold storage contracts.
  • Mixed estates behind one gate. A typical Okhla unit runs ducted or cassette cooling in the office block, heavy splits on the production floor, window units in gatehouses and stores — different duties, one contract, one consolidated report. That structure is described under commercial AC AMC.
  • Procurement-grade paperwork. Okhla businesses buy maintenance the way they buy anything — through a vendor file. GST invoicing, written scope-of-work, per-visit service records and vendor documentation are standard with every contract.

How contracts run here

Baseline assessment first — every unit tagged and measured, including the ones nobody has opened since installation. Then a schedule split by duty: production-critical units quarterly or tighter, comfort cooling on the pre-summer and post-monsoon cycle, coil cleaning at the frequency the district’s air actually demands. Breakdowns dispatch under the written 2-hour SLA from a base fifteen minutes away.

FAQ

Local specifics.

How close are you to Okhla?

Very — the Okhla industrial phases begin roughly ten to fifteen minutes from our East of Kailash office, past Nehru Place. For breakdowns, Okhla sits deep inside the 2-hour commercial SLA; for scheduled work, it is among our easiest territories to slot.

We run production floors that cannot stop. How is servicing scheduled?

Around your shifts, unit by unit — never the whole floor at once. Process-critical and server units go on quarterly or tighter cycles with drift tracked between visits; office and comfort units follow the standard calendar. Where your operation runs weekends, we do too — seven days, 7 AM to 10 PM.

Our condensers clog constantly. Is that just Okhla?

Largely, yes. Industrial exhaust, arterial road dust and dense rooftops give Okhla one of the harshest condenser environments in Delhi. The answer is shorter, measured cleaning cycles — set from the coil grades in your reports, because a dust-blinded condenser draws 15–30% more power and fails years early.
