Why installations fail early

The shortcuts are invisible on day one.

A badly installed AC cools on day one. The difference shows up in year two: gas leaks at rushed flare joints, capacity loss from un-vacuumed lines, drain problems from wrong slopes. These are the steps that prevent that:

  1. Site assessment

    Unit sizing against the room’s actual heat load, placement for airflow and serviceability, condenser location that will not choke in summer.

  2. Copper piping, done right

    Correct diameter and run, proper bends (no crushed radii), full insulation, and flare joints made with a torque wrench — the joint that leaks in year two is the one tightened by feel.

  3. Vacuum pull

    Lines evacuated with a vacuum pump — not purged with refrigerant — to remove air and moisture that corrode the system from inside.

  4. Pressure test

    The system is pressure-tested and held before any refrigerant flows. A leak found now costs minutes; found next May, it costs a gas charge and a breakdown.

  5. Commissioning, recorded

    Running pressures, current draw and air-off temperature measured and recorded at handover. Your unit starts life with a baseline on paper.

FAQ

Installation — specifics.

How long should a split AC installation take?

Three to four hours done properly: mounting, copper piping with correct bends and insulation, vacuum pull on the lines, pressure test, then commissioning with the readings recorded. An installation finished in under two hours has usually skipped the vacuum or the test — and those shortcuts surface as gas leaks and efficiency loss within a year or two.

Why does the vacuum pull matter?

Moisture and air left in refrigerant lines react with the refrigerant and oil, corrode the system from inside and reduce cooling capacity. Pulling a proper vacuum removes them. It is the single most commonly skipped step in cheap installations.

What determines split AC installation charges in Delhi?

Piping length beyond the standard run, wall type and drilling, bracket and outdoor unit placement, electrical work needed, and floor height for access. We quote after seeing the site or photos — a fixed menu price that ignores these is recovered from you later.

Do you install ducted and VRV/VRF systems?

Yes — design, installation and commissioning for ducted and VRV/VRF plant, and replacement of ageing installed systems, for commercial sites across Delhi NCR.

Do you provide commissioning documentation?

Yes. Commercial installations are handed over with the commissioning readings recorded — pressures, current draw, air-off temperatures — so the baseline exists from day one. It is the same documentation discipline as our maintenance contracts.
