The district

Connaught Place is Delhi’s original central business district: the Georgian colonnades of the Inner and Outer Circles and the radial roads between them, ringed by the corporate towers of Barakhamba Road, Kasturba Gandhi Marg and Janpath. Flagship retail, banks, airline and corporate offices, and one of the city’s densest restaurant and café concentrations share buildings that are approaching a century old.

Air Control services and contracts across the CP block and its corporate ring — about 25–35 minutes from our East of Kailash base outside peak traffic, with servicing scheduled around the district’s trading hours.

What CP’s heritage stock asks of AC maintenance

  • Buildings that predate air conditioning by decades. Thick masonry, high ceilings, deep colonnades — and not a single purpose-built AC provision. Everything is retrofit: splits over rear lanes, cassettes into false ceilings beneath heritage slabs, ducted systems threaded through upper floors. Contracts here start with a proper unit-by-unit assessment, because no two CP premises have the same installation history.
  • Condensers in service lanes and on shared terraces. The colonnade blocks push outdoor units into rear service lanes and rooftop clusters — tight, hot, dusty spaces where airflow is the first casualty. Restricted condensers run hot, draw more current and die younger; ours get placement and airflow noted in the report, and coils get shorter cleaning cycles than the Delhi average.
  • Restaurants at density. CP’s food floors combine kitchen grease load with full-occupancy evening peaks — the hardest duty cycle in comfort cooling. See AC AMC for hotels and restaurants for how those units are scheduled.
  • Brands cannot afford a hot flagship. For the retailers and banks on the Circles, a summer failure is a shuttered brand experience on Delhi’s most visible retail frontage. The pre-summer service cycle exists for exactly this stock.

How contracts run here

Retail floors are serviced from 7 AM before the Circles open; offices in zoned rotation during working hours; restaurants between services. Every unit is tagged, measured and reported per visit — and where a heritage building’s constraints (shared terraces, lane access, conservation rules on facades) limit what can be changed, the report records the constraint and the workaround, so building management decisions are made on paper rather than folklore.

FAQ

Local specifics.

Can you service outlets before trading hours?

Yes — CP work is routinely scheduled from 7 AM, before the Inner Circle opens, and restaurants are serviced between lunch and evening service or before opening. We work seven days, so closed-day servicing is also an option.

The building is heritage-listed and outdoor units sit in the service lanes. Is that a problem?

It is the normal condition of working in CP, and it shapes the job: condensers in colonnade service lanes and on shared terraces need deliberate airflow assessment and more frequent coil attention. We document placement constraints in the condition report so any remedial work is planned, not improvised.

How far is CP from your base?

About nine kilometres from East of Kailash — typically 25–35 minutes outside peak. Comfortably inside the 2-hour commercial SLA, and scheduled work is planned around the district’s hours anyway.
