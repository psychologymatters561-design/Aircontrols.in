What’s at stake

Your product is the workspace. The AC is most of it.

A coworking member who is too hot does not file a ticket and wait — they post on the community feed, mention it in the exit survey, and factor it into renewal. Comfort is the product, and it is being judged 12 hours a day by hundreds of people who chose your floor over their own office.

The estate works harder than an office estate too: longer hours, higher density, meeting rooms cycling all day, and phone booths whose small splits run continuously. High duty on a standard office maintenance calendar is how coworking floors end up hot in their second summer.

How the contract runs in coworking

Duty-based frequency
Visit cycles set from actual run hours, not a flat calendar — high-duty zones like meeting clusters checked more often.
Off-hours servicing
Work before opening or after close, seven days — members never share the floor with a service ladder.
Growth-ready inventory
New floors and reconfigurations folded into the same contract by inventory update.
Answerable SLAs
2-hour response with a named contact — so community managers answer complaints with a time, not an apology.

FAQ

Specifics for this sector.

Our floors run 12+ hours a day, sometimes weekends. Does the schedule handle that?

Yes. Long operating hours compress the maintenance windows but increase the duty on every unit — a coworking cassette can log double an office unit’s annual run hours. Servicing runs early morning or late evening (we work 7 AM to 10 PM, seven days), and visit frequency follows the actual duty, not a standard office calendar.

Members complain in the app before ops even knows. How does the contract help?

Two ways. Scheduled servicing removes the predictable complaints — the meeting room that always runs warm is usually a fouled coil or low charge, findable in February. And when something does fail, the 2-hour SLA plus a named contact gives your community team an answer with a time on it, which is most of what an annoyed member wants.

We add and reconfigure floors as we grow. Can the contract keep up?

Yes — the contract is built on a unit inventory, so expansion is an inventory update, not a renegotiation. New floors get baselined and folded into the schedule and the consolidated report.

Can reporting be split by floor or by location?

Yes. Multi-floor and multi-location operators get reporting segmented the way the business is run — per floor, per site — with one consolidated view on top. See commercial AC AMC for multi-site structure.
