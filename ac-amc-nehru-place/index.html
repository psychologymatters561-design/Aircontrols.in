The district

Nehru Place is one of Asia’s largest IT hardware markets and one of South Delhi’s densest commercial districts — a raised plaza ringed by office towers built in the late 1970s and 1980s, with hundreds of shops, service counters and offices stacked over ground-floor trading. Around the plaza sit newer anchors — the Epicuria complex at the metro station, the hotel block on the district’s edge — and the surrounding commercial pockets of Kalkaji and the approach to Okhla.

We have been maintaining air conditioning in and around this district for decades: our base in East of Kailash is about ten minutes away, and Nehru Place breakdown calls are among the fastest we can answer anywhere in Delhi.

What Nehru Place’s towers ask of AC maintenance

  • Ageing towers, layered systems. The district’s core towers are forty-plus years old. Original central plant has been variously retired, partially revived or bypassed, and floors have layered on their own splits and cassettes tenancy by tenancy. The result is exactly the kind of mixed, undocumented estate our baseline assessment was built for: inventory first, then a contract that matches what is actually on the wall.
  • Dust and heat load. An open plaza with heavy footfall, surrounded by arterial roads, feeds condensers a constant dust load; south- and west-facing frontages take full afternoon sun. Coils here foul faster than the Delhi average — and a fouled coil makes a compressor draw 15–30% more power, which on a floor of units is a standing surcharge on the electricity bill.
  • Uptime is stock and trade. The district’s businesses — component sellers, service centres, software offices, training institutes — run on footfall and screens. A hot floor in June is customers who walk back down to the plaza and buy elsewhere.
  • Power quality. Dense old buildings mean shared risers, long runs and summer voltage sags. Our protocol’s electrical checks and capacitor readings catch the damage that unstable supply does to AC electricals before it becomes a compressor failure.

How contracts run here

Ground and mezzanine trading floors are serviced before the market opens; upper-floor offices in zoned rotations during the day. Every unit is tagged and reported per visit, and the pre-summer cycle lands in February–March — before the plaza’s first 44°C afternoon finds the units with low charge and tired capacitors. Offices with an estate of cassettes and ducted plant should read AC AMC for offices; multi-floor and multi-tenancy setups, commercial AC AMC.

FAQ

Local specifics.

How quickly can you reach Nehru Place?

Our office in East of Kailash is about ten minutes from the Nehru Place plaza outside peak hours — among the closest bases any AMC contractor has to the district. The 2-hour commercial SLA applies with a lot of margin here.

Our office is in one of the older towers with a shared ducted system plus our own splits. What can a contract cover?

Your own estate — the splits and cassettes your office installed — on a full contract, with per-unit reports. Where the building’s shared plant affects your floor, the report documents what your units are compensating for, which is useful evidence with building management.

Can servicing happen around trading hours?

Yes. Shops and service counters are done before the market opens; upper-floor offices during working hours zone by zone. We work 7 AM to 10 PM, seven days, so the calendar fits the district’s rhythm.
