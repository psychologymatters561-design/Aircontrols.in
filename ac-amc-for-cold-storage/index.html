What’s at stake

Downtime here is measured in inventory, not comfort.

In an office, a failed AC is a bad afternoon. In cold storage, hours of drift above the band can write off stock worth more than a decade of maintenance contracts — and the failure mode is rarely sudden. Plant that runs 24 hours a day, 365 days a year does not get the overnight rest cycles comfort cooling gets; wear accumulates continuously, and small deviations compound quietly until a compressor lets go on a June afternoon.

That is why temperature-critical plant is maintained on measurement, not on complaint: by the time anyone feels the problem in a cold room, the inventory has been feeling it for hours.

How the contract runs in cold storage

High-duty schedule
Quarterly or monthly visits for 24/7 plant — condensers, refrigerant charge, electricals and controls checked on cycle, not annually.
Drift tracking
Pressures and current draw trended visit to visit; a unit working harder to hold the same set point is flagged before it fails.
No-downtime servicing
Unit-by-unit work in agreed windows; standby and redundancy verified as part of the visit.
Audit-ready records
Signed, dated per-unit service records for the cold-chain compliance file.

FAQ

Specifics for this sector.

Our plant runs 24/7. How does servicing work without downtime?

Unit by unit, never all at once, in windows agreed with your operations team — and where the site has redundancy, it is verified as part of the visit so the standby capacity is real, not assumed. Visit frequency for 24/7 duty plant is quarterly or monthly, not the comfort-cooling two visits a year.

Can you support cold-chain compliance documentation?

The contract produces written per-unit service and condition records — what was measured, what was found, what was done, signed and dated — which is the maintenance-side paper trail cold-chain audits ask for. The record format is here.

What does 'drift' mean and why does the report track it?

Drift is a unit still holding its set point but working progressively harder to do it — pressures moving away from spec, current draw creeping up, run time lengthening. On comfort cooling, drift is a bigger bill. On temperature-critical plant, drift is the early warning before the failure that costs you inventory. Tracking it is the point of measuring instead of eyeballing.

Do you cover both the cold rooms and the ancillary areas?

Yes — temperature-critical plant on high-duty schedules, plus the office, dispatch and staff areas on standard cycles, under one contract with one consolidated report.
